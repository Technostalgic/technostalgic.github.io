Our Solar System at a Glance

A one-page fact sheet about the eight planets, adapted from NASA Science.

Our Cosmic Neighborhood

Our solar system is located in the Milky Way, a barred spiral galaxy with two major arms, and two minor arms. The four planets closest to the Sun are small and rocky. The four planets farthest from the Sun are giant worlds made mostly of gas and ice.

Our solar system includes the Sun, eight planets, five officially named dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, and thousands of asteroids and comets.

- Solar System Facts, NASA Science

Inner Planets

Mercury

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun, and the smallest planet in our solar system. It's only slightly larger than Earth's Moon. It orbits about 0.39 AU from the Sun.

Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun, and the sixth largest planet. It's the hottest planet in our solar system. It orbits about 0.72 AU from the Sun.

Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only world known to support life. By definition, it orbits 1 AU from the Sun.

Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun, and the seventh largest. It orbits about 1.52 AU from the Sun.

Did you know?

Mars is the only planet we know of inhabited entirely by robots.

Outer Planets

Jupiter

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun, and the largest in the solar system, by far - more than twice as massive as the other planets combined.

Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun, and the second largest in the solar system. It's surrounded by beautiful rings.

Uranus

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun, and the third largest planet in our solar system. It appears to spin sideways. William Herschel discovered it on March 13, 1781.

Neptune

Neptune is the eighth, and most distant planet from the Sun. It's the fourth-largest, and the first planet discovered with math. Astronomers first saw it through a telescope on September 23, 1846.

Pluto

What happened to Pluto?

Pluto was long considered our ninth planet, but the International Astronomical Union reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet in 2006. The vote took place on August 24, 2006.

Glossary

Orbit
The curved path an object takes around a star, planet or moon.
Astronomical Unit (AU)
The average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 149.6 million kilometers.
Dwarf planet
A round object that orbits the Sun but has not cleared other objects out of its orbit, like Pluto.
Gas giant
A large planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium, like Jupiter and Saturn.
Ice giant
A large planet made mostly of water, ammonia and methane ices, like Uranus and Neptune.
